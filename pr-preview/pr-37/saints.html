Saints & Blesseds

Holy men and women of the Trinitarian Family

The Order’s calendar honors five canonized saints, a company of blesseds, and many venerables and servants of God. Their lives show the charism in different states of life: founder, reformer, preacher, mystic, wife, mother, and martyr.

Canonized saints

St. John de Matha

Founder · 17 December

Born at Faucon in Provence around 1154, John studied and taught in Paris before his ordination. At his first Mass on 28 January 1193 he received the vision of Christ between two captives that gave the Order its mission and its cross. He gathered the first community at Cerfroid, obtained the approval of Innocent III in 1198, and died in Rome on 17 December 1213.

St. Felix de Valois

Co-founder according to tradition · 4 November

A hermit at Cerfroid, Felix joined John de Matha’s project and is venerated as co-founder. The Order acknowledges that the documentary evidence for his life is weaker than for John’s, while honoring his role in the community’s contemplative dimension. He died around 1212.

St. John Baptist of the Conception

Reformer · 14 February

Born at Almodóvar del Campo in 1561, he entered the Trinitarians at Toledo and became a celebrated preacher. He led the Spanish reform, approved by Clement VIII in 1599, and founded numerous discalced houses. He died at Córdoba on 14 February 1613 and was canonized in 1975.

St. Simon de Rojas

Priest · 28 September

Born at Valladolid around 1552, Simon spent most of his Trinitarian life in teaching and government. He was a sought-after spiritual director and a tireless servant of the poor, and he promoted devotion to the Holy Name of Mary. He founded the Congregation of the Slaves of the Holy Name of Mary in Madrid in 1611. He died in 1624 and was canonized in 1988.

St. Michael of the Saints

Priest · 8 June

Born at Vic in 1591, Michael entered the Trinitarians as a young man and later joined the reformed branch. He was known for deep contemplation, joyful charity, and a special love for the young. He died at Valladolid in 1625 and was canonized in 1862. He is protector of Trinitarian youth.

Blesseds

Bl. Mark Criado

Priest and martyr · 24 September

Born at Andújar around 1522, Mark was a Trinitarian preacher in the Alpujarras. He was killed for the faith at La Peza in 1569 during the Morisco revolt. He was beatified in 1899.

Bl. Elisabetta Canori Mora

Wife, mother, Trinitarian tertiary · 4 February

Born in Rome in 1774, Elisabetta married and raised four daughters amid great suffering. She combined prayer and works of mercy with fidelity to her family and was beatified in 1994. Her diary is a notable spiritual witness.

Bl. Anna Maria Taigi

Wife, mother, Trinitarian tertiary · 9 June

Born at Siena in 1769, Anna Maria married in Rome and had seven children. She cared for the poor and sick, advised families, and was known for deep prayer and discernment. She died in 1837 and was beatified in 1920.

Bl. Dominic Iturrate of the Blessed Sacrament

Priest · 11 May

Born at Dima in 1901, Dominic entered the Trinitarians and was ordained in 1925. Tuberculosis ended his planned missionary work, and he died at Belmonte in 1927 at the age of twenty-five. He was beatified in 1983.

Bl. Frances of the Incarnation

Virgin and martyr · 13 January

María Francisca Espejo Martos was born at Martos in 1873 and professed as a Trinitarian sister in 1894. She was arrested during the Spanish persecution and killed on the night of 12–13 January 1937.

Bl. Innocent XI

Pope, Trinitarian tertiary · 12 August

Benedetto Odescalchi was born at Como in 1611, became a cardinal, and was elected pope in 1676. He was a reformer of the Church’s government and a benefactor of the Trinitarian mission. He died in 1689.

Bl. Mariano of St. Joseph and Companions

Martyrs · 6 November

Trinitarian martyrs of the Spanish Civil War from the regions of Cuenca and Jaén. They were beatified in 2007. The Order also honors other groups of twentieth-century martyrs, including those beatified in 2013.

The Order’s calendar also lists several blesseds and saints who were members of Trinitarian confraternities or tertiaries rather than friars. They are noted under patrons and associates below. The confraternity named in these entries is the historical Confraternity of the Most Holy Trinity, not the Third Order. See Confraternities of the Most Holy Trinity.

Patrons and associates

  • Our Lady of Good Remedy, principal patroness of the Order (8 October).
  • St. Agnes, principal patroness of the Order (28 January), honored on the anniversary of the founding vision.
  • St. Louis IX, King of France, Trinitarian tertiary and patron associated with the Order (25 August).
  • St. John de Ribera, bishop and member of the Confraternity of the Most Holy Trinity (7 January).
  • St. Vincent Pallotti, priest and Trinitarian tertiary (22 January).
  • St. Benedict Joseph Labre, member of the Confraternity of the Most Holy Trinity (16 April).
  • Bl. Diego José de Cádiz, Capuchin and member of the Confraternity (24 March).

These figures are not all members of the Trinitarian Order. The distinctions are given so that patrons and associates are not confused with the Order’s own saints and blesseds.

Venerables and servants of God

The Church has also recognized a wider company of Trinitarian men and women whose causes are at earlier stages. Among them are:

  • Venerable Giuseppe Di Donna (1901–1952), bishop of Andria and missionary in Madagascar.
  • Venerable Francisco de Asís Méndez Casariego (1850–1924), founder of the Trinitarian Sisters of Madrid.
  • Venerable Mariana Allsopp González-Manrique (1854–1933), co-foundress of the Trinitarian Sisters of Madrid.
  • Venerable Angela Maria Autsch (1900–1944), Trinitarian Sister of Valence and martyr of the Nazi regime.
  • Venerable Félix Monasterio Ateca (1902–1951), Trinitarian priest.
  • Servant of God Maria Teresa Cucchiari (1734–1801), foundress of the Trinitarian Sisters of Rome.

This is a selected list, not a complete catalogue of causes. Titles and dates should be checked against the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints and the Order’s postulation.
